Crossing the Atlantic: 28 Days Between Wind and Water

By Serena Marchetti · 12 min read

The first thing you lose is your sense of time. Within a week at sea, the familiar rhythm of hours dissolves into something older and more honest: the arc of the sun, the shift of the stars, the watch schedule that divides your crew into sleeping halves.

"There is nothing between you and the horizon. That is not emptiness — it is space enough for every thought you have been avoiding."

By day four we had left the shipping lanes. The AIS display, which had shown a constellation of nearby vessels, cleared to a single blinking dot: ours. We were, for the first time in our lives, genuinely alone on open water.

The Crew

Four people share this 42-foot sloop. Serena handles navigation; Diego manages the sails with a quiet competence that borders on art; Yuki and Tomás split the cooking and the overnight watches. Each brings a different temperament to the confined space of a 42-foot hull, which turns out to be exactly enough.
